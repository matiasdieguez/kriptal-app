How does Kriptal ensure end-to-end encryption?

By design, Kriptal provides end-to-end encryption for messages exchanged between Kriptal identities. The sender’s device encrypts each message for the recipient before creating the shareable file; there’s no Kriptal message server or relay. The message also carries a sender signature. The implementation combines P-256 ECDH with ML-KEM-768 to derive the encryption key, and uses ML-DSA-65 for signatures (crypto.js, message creation). It is designed to resist post-quantum attacks, but I wouldn’t claim it is proven “post-quantum safe.” ML-KEM-768 and ML-DSA-65 are post-quantum algorithms, and the message key combines ML-KEM with classical P-256. However, the whole application has not been independently audited or formally verified. Kriptal also does not provide a ratchet or forward secrecy: if a recipient’s long-term private keys are later compromised, previously saved message files may be at risk. For practical security, verify a contact’s key fingerprint through a trusted separate channel, protect your devices and vault password, and remember that a compromised browser or app update could undermine encryption. These limits are also described in the legal notice.
