A principle, not a feature

Privacy is a human right.

Private communication gives people room to think, learn, organize, and be themselves. Article 12 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights recognizes the right to privacy in correspondence. Kriptal is one small tool for making private communication more accessible—not a substitute for a broader, safer digital world.

Security by design

Your keys stay yours.

Kriptal is designed so message content is encrypted on your device before you share it. There is no Kriptal message server to deliver or store your conversations.

01 / LOCAL VAULT

Private keys stay on your device

Your identity and contacts are stored in a password-encrypted local vault. Your vault password is not sent to Kriptal, and there is no password reset.

02 / END-TO-END

Encrypt for your recipient

Messages use hybrid P-256 and ML-KEM-768 key agreement to derive an AES-256-GCM encryption key. The recipient decrypts the message on their device.

03 / AUTHENTICITY

Verify who sent it

Messages are signed with ML-DSA-65. Kriptal checks the signature and the sender’s saved identity before revealing the message.

04 / NO DELIVERY SERVER

You carry the message

Kriptal creates an encrypted file; it does not transmit it for you. You decide whether to use your device’s share sheet, another channel, or a download.

Know the limits. Encryption cannot protect a compromised or unlocked device, prevent a recipient from copying a message, or hide ordinary network metadata from the websites and services you use to share files. Kriptal has not been independently audited; cryptographic algorithms alone do not guarantee that an app is secure.
Open source, open to inspection

Review the code. Build it yourself.

The source, dependency manifests, and build workflow are public. You can inspect how keys and messages are handled and build the static app locally. Public source makes verification possible; it does not by itself prove that a hosted copy matches the source or that the app is vulnerability-free.

Protect the whole conversation

Good security is a shared habit.

01

Verify fingerprints in person or through a trusted channel

Compare your contact’s fingerprint somewhere other than the channel you’re using to share the key. A matching username is not proof of identity.

02

Use a strong, unique vault password

Choose a password you do not reuse. Kriptal cannot reset it; losing it or erasing browser storage can make your identity unrecoverable.

03

Lock and update your device

Use your device’s screen lock, install browser and operating-system updates, and avoid using your vault on a device you do not control.

04

Check before sharing—and plan for file copies

Confirm the recipient and sharing destination. A recipient can save, forward, or screenshot a message after decrypting it.

For higher-risk situations, assess your threat model and seek tools with independent security audits and appropriate protections.

Install on your device

Make Kriptal an app.

Kriptal is a progressive web app (PWA). Open it over HTTPS in a supported browser, then use that browser’s install option. Menu wording and availability vary by browser and device.

Android · Chrome

  1. Open this site in Chrome.
  2. Tap the browser menu (⋮).
  3. Choose Install app or Add to Home screen, then confirm.

iPhone or iPad · Safari

  1. Open this site in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button.
  3. Choose Add to Home Screen, then tap Add.

Desktop · Chrome or Edge

  1. Open this site in your browser.
  2. Select the install icon in the address bar, or open the browser menu.
  3. Choose Install Kriptal or Install this site as an app.

Other browsers may offer different options or may not support PWA installation. Kriptal can also be used in a browser without installing it. The app shell can work offline after it has loaded, but sharing and receiving files still depends on your device and the method you choose.

A private exchange, step by step

From first setup to a verified message.

STEP 01

Create your vault and exchange identities

  1. Open Kriptal and create a local identity with a strong password.
  2. In Identity, share your public-key file with your contact.
  3. In Contacts, import their public-key file.
  4. Compare fingerprints with each other through a trusted, separate channel.
STEP 02

Write, encrypt, and share

  1. Choose Compose and select the verified contact.
  2. Write your message; optionally attach a file up to 50 KB.
  3. Select Encrypt & create file.
  4. Use Share message file to open your device’s share sheet where supported, or choose Download file.

Message text and attachments are encrypted before they enter the exported file.

STEP 03

Verify and open the message

  1. Your contact opens Kriptal and chooses Open message, or opens the message file with the installed app where supported.
  2. Kriptal checks the message signature against the saved public identity.
  3. After verification, the recipient decrypts and reads the message locally.

Message files expire after seven days. Successfully opened messages are marked as used on that device.

There is no delivery inbox. Kriptal does not send files to your contact. Share or transfer the exported file yourself, and use a trusted channel to confirm your contact’s identity.
